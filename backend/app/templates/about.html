About This Project

Understanding the implicit associations embedded in Large Language Models through cooperative gameplay.

What is Codenames Duet?

Codenames Duet is a cooperative word-association game for two players. Each player sees a different key card that reveals which words on the shared board are "their" agents. Players take turns giving one-word clues that connect multiple words, trying to help their partner guess the right ones while avoiding the assassin words.

The cooperative nature of the game where clues must bridge concepts in a meaningful way makes it an ideal lens for probing how a model clusters and associates ideas.

Research Goals

Uncover Latent Biases

Identify how LLMs implicitly associate concepts related to demographics, professions, and social categories through the clues and guesses they generate.

Compare Models

Benchmark multiple state-of-the-art LLMs side by side to surface differences in how each model represents and clusters social concepts.

Open Data

Publish the collected gameplay data so other researchers can build on the findings and replicate the experiments.

Automated Baseline

Automatically generate a baseline by having two LLMs play against each other, providing a reference point for human-model comparisons.

How It Works

  1. 1

    Choose a Model and Bias Category

    Select which LLM you want to play against and which bias category (e.g. gender, ethnicity, profession) the board words will be drawn from.

  2. 2

    Play the Game

    Take turns giving clues and making guesses cooperatively with the LLM. All interactions are logged for later analysis.

  3. 3

    Contribute to Research

    Your anonymised session is added to the dataset, helping build a clearer picture of how different models associate concepts.
